## Idea

Reading (fan and critical interpretations found by web search; nothing official from the band): on *Hawaii:
Part II* a young man, Simon, finds his lover dead in a forest. In "Murders" he panics, explains himself in a rush as
if he already knows he will be blamed, and the song ends on a quiet, regretful piano. The album goes on to his
accusation and confinement.

The video stages that as a **toy paper theatre at night**. Simon and the girl are paper-doll puppets with brass
split-pin joints. The forest is cut-paper layers, and the owls are the witnesses whose eyes snap open on the drum
hits. Hanging mirrors multiply Simon's face as panic sets in, gilded picture frames slam around him (he is *framed*),
and an owl court passes judgement. Then the theatre itself tears apart, and snow falls on an empty clearing. One
colour is never paper-pale: **the red ribbon** (hers, then his).

It is never shown who did it. The death is implied (she lies among white paper flowers, eyes closed); there is no
violence or blood. No lyrics appear on screen; the only words are act cards, the title and the song credit.
